 3.3.2. Effect of pyrolysis temperature on the TPN production
 The catalytic pyrolysis process from PET to TPN should undergo a
 series of reactions, including PET decomposition, urea decomposition,
 ammonization of pyrolysis intermediates, dehydration reactions, and
 other cracking side reactions that were highly affected by the pyrolysis
 temperature. Therefore, pyrolysis temperature is another key important
 factor in TPN production. Herein, the effect of pyrolysis temperatures
 from 500 to 700 ◦ C on the production of TPN and aromatic nitriles was
 investigated in detail.
 Fig. 5 indicates that pyrolysis temperature had an obvious effect on
 the distribution of pyrolysis products. Higher pyrolysis led to more
 intense cracking reactions and resulted in less coke and more gas
 products. In addition, the products, such as nitriles and aromatics,
 collected in the cold trap were also significantly affected by the pyrolysis
 temperature. The maximum carbon yield of nitriles was obtained at
 550 ◦ C and reached 38.7 C%. The carbon yield of nitriles started to
 decrease with the pyrolysis temperature further increasing, which was
 only about 17 C% at 700 ◦ C. However, for the aromatics, the higher
 pyrolysis temperature resulted in higher carbon yields of aromatics.
 While the pyrolysis temperature reached 700 ◦ C, the carbon yield of
 aromatics reached about 20 C%. This could be caused by the decar­
 boxylation and deamidation reactions of the products and intermediates
 (such as aromatic esters, aromatic amides, and aromatic nitriles)

 4. Conclusions

 This study has successfully synthesized aromatic nitriles, particularly
 TPN, through in situ catalytic pyrolysis of waste PET plastic employing
 urea as an active ammonia source and commercial γ-Al2O3 catalyst.
 Results of TG-DTG and DAEM kinetic analysis demonstrated that posi­
 tive interaction between the waste PET and urea led to facilitated
 thermal decomposition. Urea dosage and pyrolysis temperature were
 pivotal in achieving optimal TPN production. Under 550 ◦ C and urea
 equivalent to PET, a TPN yield of 32.5% with 85% selectivity was ob­
 tained. Furthermore, DFT calculations corroborated these findings by
 affirming the role of active ammonia in lowering energy barriers and
 facilitating PET depolymerization, resulting in the formation of aromatic
 amides and subsequent dehydration to produce aromatic nitriles. The
 study presents a novel approach method for recycling waste PET into
 valuable aromatic nitriles through in situ catalytic pyrolysis with urea
 and enhances the understanding of the pyrolysis process. Furthermore,
 the pyrolysis process aligns with the principles of the circular economy
 and the growing emphasis on reducing plastic waste, thereby demon­

aromatic nitriles in the catalytic pyrolysis process. chemicals.
 Looking forward, further research should be carried out in multiple
 directions to ensure the successful application of this technology. Firstly,
 it is important to explore different catalyst materials that are more stable
 and efficient, as well as to further optimize the parameters of in situ
 catalytic pyrolysis for the industrial production of aromatic nitriles from
 PET. Additionally, thorough studies on the scale-up and industrializa­
 tion potential of the proposed in situ catalytic pyrolysis process, which
 includes techno-economic analysis, process optimization, and assess­
 ment of environmental impact, are essential. Furthermore, it is impor­
 tant to broaden the scope of the study to encompass a wider range of
 waste plastics beyond PET, and to assess the potential of catalytic py­
 rolysis with active ammonia for recycling other waste plastics into
 valuable N-containing compounds. By addressing these research di­
 rections, further progress can be achieved in the field of sustainable
 plastic waste management and the production of high-value chemicals,
 thus contributing to both environmental and economic sustainability.
